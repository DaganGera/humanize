# Profile: cover letter, statement of purpose, personal statement

No built-in baseline. Use anything the person wrote unaided (an earlier statement, long emails): `H baseline own.txt -o voice.json`.

Readers of applications see hundreds of model-written ones. What separates a real one is material only the applicant has, so step 4 of the skill (asking the author) is most of the work here.

## What a human application looks like

It names things: the project, the tool, the result with its number, the person worked with, the paper or product at the destination and why that one. It has an uneven shape because a real history is uneven. It states what the applicant wants in plain words.

## How model-written applications differ

Passion and fascination declared in the first line. Qualities claimed in threes ("dedicated, curious and resilient"). Each paragraph ends on what the experience "taught me". The destination is praised in terms that fit any institution. The closing expresses confidence and eagerness.

## Rewriting

1. Ask for the specifics before writing anything: what exactly was built or studied, the result, what went wrong, why this place (a named group, course, product or person), what they want to do there.
2. Replace every claimed quality with the event that shows it. Cut the quality word.
3. Cut any sentence that would be true in another applicant's letter.
4. Plain first person, past tense for what was done, one sentence for what is wanted.
5. Keep the applicant's own phrasing where it is clear, including non-native phrasing. Fix errors only.
6. Stay inside the word limit and keep required headings.

Never invent or inflate an achievement, a grade, a title or a date.
