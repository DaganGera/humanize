# Profile: conversation, chat reply, forum comment

Reference: 1,721 transcripts of unscripted human speech (HAP-E corpus). It is a rough stand-in: typed chat is shorter and looser still. The person's own past messages are a much better reference.

## What human conversation looks like

- Length swings widely (standard deviation about 84% of the mean). Four in ten sentences are 10 words or fewer. Fragments are normal.
- Contractions at 39 per 1,000 words. First person at 44 per 1,000. Questions are frequent.
- Short words (4.1 letters). `so`, `and`, `but`, `just`, `really`, `like`, `I think`.
- One thing per message. No structure.

## How model-written replies differ

They answer more than was asked, open by acknowledging the question, close by offering more help, and arrive in complete balanced sentences with lists and bold. Sentences with no comma almost disappear (effect size above 3 standard deviations against speech). Hedges are stacked for safety.

## Rewriting

1. Answer the question in the first words. Then stop, or add the one thing the other person needs next.
2. Match the other person's length and register. A one-line question gets a one-line answer.
3. No greeting that restates the question, no closing offer, no summary.
4. No headings, bullets or bold in a message unless the content is a list the person asked for.
5. Fragments and contractions are fine. So is `I don't know`.
6. Do not fake sloppiness. No invented typos or slang the person does not use.
