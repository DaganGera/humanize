# Profile: work email and messages

No built-in baseline. Ask for 5 to 10 emails the person wrote themselves and build one: `H baseline sent.txt -o voice.json`. Without samples, use `H measure` for the hot spots only and follow the rules below.

## What human work email looks like

Short. The request or the news comes first. It assumes shared context and names the specific thing (the file, the date, the amount, the person). Tone follows the relationship: terse with close colleagues, fuller with strangers. Sign-offs are habitual and brief.

## How model-written email differs

It opens with a wish for the reader's well-being, restates context the reader already has, wraps the request in appreciation, lists three balanced points, and closes by offering further help. Every sentence is complete and polite to the same degree, whoever the reader is.

## Rewriting

1. First line: what you need or what happened. Second: the detail needed to act (date, file, number, name).
2. Cut context the reader has. Cut thanks that do not refer to something specific they did.
3. One ask per email where possible, with a date if there is one.
4. Keep the person's own greeting and sign-off habits. A sign-off is not a tell.
5. Match the warmth of the thread. Do not add enthusiasm.
6. No bold, no headings, bullets only for real lists.

Never change names, amounts, dates or commitments. Never soften or strengthen a refusal.
